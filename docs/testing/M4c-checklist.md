# OpenDrape M4c: what to try

This build changes how fabric behaves. Fabric now keeps off fabric (sleeves rest on the
bodice, a hem can't fall through the front), seams weld flat instead of creasing, the
fabric is made on its grain (warp and weft along the cells, bias on the diagonals, which
gives), and a **Fabric detail** setting trades detail for speed. Open the T-shirt project
you drafted for M4b (or draft it again from that checklist).

## Check these

- [ ] **Sleeves rest on the bodice.** Press **Play**. Once the seams have shut and the shirt
      hangs, orbit round: the sleeves sit on the bodice at the armholes and the hem hangs
      clear. Before this build a sleeve passed through the bodice. (One small fold at the
      top of a cap, where three seams meet, may still poke through: that is a known limit.)
- [ ] **Seams are flat.** Look along the side seams and the shoulder seams: a smooth join,
      no sharp crease and no zigzag. The seams shut one at a time as each pulls closed, in
      about half a second, with gravity starting just after.
- [ ] **Fabric has a bias.** Draft a 30 × 30 cm square, press **Play**, and grab a corner
      with the pointer and pull sideways: it gives diagonally (shears), and pulling along an
      edge barely stretches it.
- [ ] **Fabric detail.** Open **View → Fabric detail**: Auto says which preset this computer
      gets (Draft on two cores or fewer, Normal otherwise). Pick **Draft (20 mm)**, press
      **Play** again: the overlay's "points" falls to about a third and "ms per step" drops.
      Pick **Fine (8 mm)**: more points, smaller folds, about twice Normal's time. The choice
      is remembered next time you open the app.
- [ ] **A seam that can't close is said so.** Place the front at the front and the back at
      the back as usual, then drag the back piece round to the front so the two pieces
      overlap, and press **Play**. After three seconds the seams are pulled shut anyway and a
      note says which seam couldn't close on its own. **Reset**, place the back at the back,
      and it drapes normally.
- [ ] **A settled drape costs nothing.** Leave a finished drape on screen: the overlay's
      "ms per step" falls once nothing moves, and the simulation pauses itself after a
      second of stillness as before.
- [ ] **Edits while draping still carry on**, at the same detail: with a drape running,
      lengthen the hem; the drape is made again and carries on from where it was.

## If something looks wrong

Use **Help → Copy diagnostics** and send the text, with what you did and what you saw.
